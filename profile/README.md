## Research & Development

ESTER brings together several interconnected areas of scientific work. Our GitHub repositories document the processes through which archaeological and palaeoenvironmental evidence is transformed into quantitative population reconstructions.

### Data & Evidence

We assemble, evaluate, and integrate archaeological and palaeoenvironmental evidence relevant to prehistoric population dynamics.

Our research draws on multiple complementary sources, including:

- **Radiocarbon dates** — chronological evidence for past human activity.
- **Archaeological site records** — settlement evidence and changes in archaeological visibility.
- **Dendrochronological records** — precisely dated evidence of human activity.
- **Palaeoenvironmental indicators** — evidence of landscape change, vegetation, and human environmental impact.
- **Expert knowledge** — independent population estimates and constraints derived from archaeological research.

Our work includes data harmonisation, the treatment of chronological uncertainty, provenance documentation, and reproducible workflows for preparing heterogeneous evidence for demographic modelling.

### Models & Methods

ESTER develops a **Bayesian hierarchical modelling framework** for reconstructing prehistoric population dynamics from heterogeneous and incomplete evidence.

A central objective is to distinguish between:

1. **Latent demographic processes** — the underlying population dynamics we aim to reconstruct.
2. **Observation processes** — the mechanisms through which demographic activity generates archaeological and environmental evidence.
3. **Uncertainty and variability** — arising from the archaeological record, measurement processes, model assumptions, and regional differences.

By integrating multiple proxies within a common probabilistic framework, ESTER aims to develop population reconstructions that explicitly account for uncertainty and differences in data quality.

### Software & Reproducible Research

We develop computational tools, analytical pipelines, and reproducible research workflows supporting:

- Data preparation and integration.
- Statistical inference and demographic reconstruction.
- Model validation and sensitivity analysis.
- Regional case studies and comparative analyses.
- Visualisation, documentation, and communication of results.

Code and research outputs will be released with appropriate documentation, versioning, and licensing as they become ready for public use.

**Explore our work:** [All ESTER repositories](https://github.com/orgs/project-ester-hub/repositories)

### Scientific Documentation

Alongside our research publications, we are developing documentation of the scientific and computational foundations of ESTER.

This includes descriptions of our modelling framework, proxy-specific observation models, data sources, analytical methods, and reproducible workflows.

Documentation currently resides primarily in the respective repositories. A dedicated scientific and methodological documentation portal is planned.

### Open Science & Collaboration

Our intention is to make research data, analytical workflows, statistical models, and software publicly available under appropriate licenses, subject to applicable data access and ethical constraints.

We encourage collaboration with researchers across prehistoric archaeology, palaeoenvironmental science, statistics, computational modelling, and related disciplines.

### How to Contribute

We welcome engagement from researchers, developers, and practitioners interested in our work.

If you would like to contribute:

1. **Explore** the relevant repository and its available documentation.
2. **Check** existing issues and discussions before proposing changes.
3. **Open an issue** to report problems, suggest improvements, or initiate a technical discussion.
4. **Consult our standards** before contributing code, data, or research workflows.
5. **Discuss substantial contributions** with the relevant ESTER team members, particularly where scientific interpretation, data ownership, or authorship may be involved.

Our approach to intellectual contributions, acknowledgment, and co-authorship is outlined in the [Collaboration & Attribution Policy](#collaboration--attribution-policy).

## About the Research

ESTER — *Estimating Eurasia's Prehistoric Population* — is an interdisciplinary research initiative combining three complementary areas of expertise:

| Research Area | Contribution |
|:---|:---|
| **Prehistoric Archaeology** | Material and chronological evidence for past human activity, settlement, and societal change. |
| **Palaeoenvironmental Science** | Independent evidence of landscape transformation, vegetation dynamics, and environmental conditions. |
| **Computational Modelling** | Statistical integration of heterogeneous evidence into quantitative, uncertainty-aware population reconstructions. |

Our research focuses on prehistoric population dynamics across **Europe and Western Asia between 12,000 and 2,000 BP**.

Rather than relying on any single archaeological proxy, ESTER investigates how different sources of evidence can be combined within a common statistical framework.

This involves examining the relationships between demographic processes, archaeological observations, environmental records, and the uncertainties associated with each.

The project aims to produce quantitative reconstructions of past population dynamics and investigate their relationships with technological innovations, environmental changes, and societal transformations.

**Host Institution:** [Kiel University](https://www.uni-kiel.de/), [Institute of Pre- and Protohistoric Archaeology](https://www.ufg.uni-kiel.de/en?set_language=en)

**Funding:** European Research Council (ERC), Consolidator Grant

For an overview of our research questions, activities, and project developments, visit the **[ESTER Project Website](https://ester-project.org)**.

### Collaboration & Attribution Policy

The ESTER project team is open to collaboration and welcomes engagement from researchers, developers, and practitioners interested in our work.

When substantial intellectual contributions are provided by members of the ESTER team, we kindly ask for appropriate credit.

Intellectual contributions may include, but are not limited to:

- Development of custom code or software components.
- Data processing, manipulation, or integration.
- Methodological support, including experimental design, modelling approaches, or analytical strategies.

The form of credit will be determined through mutual agreement, reflecting the nature and extent of contributions. This may include co-authorship on manuscripts, datasets, or other research outputs arising from collaborative efforts.
